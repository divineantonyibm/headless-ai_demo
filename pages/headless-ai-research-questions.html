01

Guardrails & policy — agent behaviour comprehension

Does the Policy Studio help users understand what the agent can do, what it is blocked from doing, and how much control the user actually holds?

Primary research questions
RQ-1A

What understanding do IT operators and compliance officers develop about the agent's autonomous scope when reviewing the Policy Studio permission matrix — and does that understanding accurately reflect what the agent will and will not do?

  • Without being told, can you describe what this agent is allowed to do on its own right now — and what requires your sign-off?
  • Where on this screen does it tell you the consequences of each permission level — not just the label?
  • If you had to hand this configuration to a colleague, what would you warn them about?
Policy Studio Extends Task 4 RO-5
RQ-1B

How accurately do users predict the agent's behaviour in an escalation scenario after reading the permission matrix — and what gaps exist between their mental model and the intended policy logic?

  • Given this permission matrix, what do you think the agent would do if it detected a BGP policy violation at 2 am with no human online?
  • The "Modify BGP policy" row says "Approval" — does that mean the action is blocked or queued? Walk me through your reasoning.
  • Is there anything on this screen that surprised you about how much autonomy the agent has?
Net new Policy Studio Mental model accuracy
RQ-1C

What do users understand "Approval" versus "Ask" to mean in terms of the agent's runtime behaviour — and do they perceive a meaningful distinction between the two?

  • What's the difference between a permission set to "Ask" and one set to "Approval"? Can you describe what each looks like in practice?
  • Which of those two modes feels like the agent is more in control? More like you are?
  • If you set everything to "Approval", what do you expect would happen to the agent's usefulness?
Net new Permission model Vocabulary legibility
02

Change impact communication

When a user adjusts a guardrail setting, does the system communicate what will change — and is that feedback specific enough to support a confident decision?

Primary research questions
RQ-2A

When IT operators and compliance officers adjust a permission level in the Policy Studio, what signals do they use to understand the downstream impact — and is the posture radar sufficient to support a confident save decision?

  • You just changed "Restart pods" from Auto to Ask. What changed on the screen? What does that change mean to you in practice?
  • Looking at the posture radar now versus before — what specifically changed, and is that what you expected?
  • Would you feel confident saving this policy right now? What information is missing before you'd click Save?
Extends Task 4 Posture radar RO-5
RQ-2B

Do users notice and correctly interpret the composite score movement when they modify a permission — and do they treat it as a meaningful gate or as a decorative indicator?

  • The composite score changed from 85 to 71 after your last edit. What does that drop tell you?
  • Is there a score below which you would stop and not save the policy?
  • What would you need to understand about how the score is calculated before you trusted it as a decision signal?
Net new Composite score Trust in metric
RQ-2C

What kind of impact preview — if any — do users expect to see before committing a policy change, and how does the absence of a "what happens next" summary affect their confidence?

  • Before you save this policy, what would you want the system to show you — for example, a list of upcoming agent actions this change would affect?
  • Is there a type of change where you'd want a mandatory review step before saving — for example, anything touching network permissions?
  • How does this compare to making a similar change in a tool you use today — what does that tool show you before you confirm?
Net new Change preview Expectation gap
03

System health communication

Does the Canvas dashboard give users a clear, accurate sense of whether the system is healthy — and do they know what to do when it is not?

Primary research questions
RQ-3A

What story do operators construct about overall system health from the Canvas dashboard, and which specific elements drive their assessment — before any explanation is given?

  • In one sentence, tell me: is the system healthy right now? What made you say that?
  • Which number or visual element was the first thing you looked at to form that judgment?
  • Is "95% confidence" a health indicator, a risk indicator, or something else to you?
Extends Task 1 Canvas dashboard RO-2
RQ-3B

When the system shows a degraded signal — a failing badge, a declined confidence score, or a "Fix failed" case — do operators understand what action they are expected to take, and do they know where to go next?

  • You see "Fix failed" on a case. What does that mean — the agent gave up? A human needs to act? Something else?
  • If fleet health dropped from 94% to 74% overnight, how would you find out what caused it? Can you show me the path on this screen?
  • Is there anything on this dashboard that tells you what to do when something goes wrong — not just that it went wrong?
Net new Degraded state Actionability
RQ-3C

Do users understand the relationship between the ambient health indicators (fleet health, confidence score) and the individual agent activity cards — and do they perceive the dashboard as one coherent system view?

  • These two cards in the right rail are about specific incidents. How do they relate to the 94% fleet health at the top?
  • If both of these incidents were resolved, what do you think would happen to the health score?
  • Is there a health or status view you'd want that you don't see here? What would it show?
Net new Ambient monitoring Coherence of view
04

Human-in-the-loop sufficiency

When the system pulls the user in for a decision — approve, escalate, deny — is the information shown sufficient to act confidently, without needing to leave the screen?

Primary research questions
RQ-4A

When operators and compliance officers are presented with an escalation in the Case Detail view, can they make a confident approve or deny decision using only the information on screen — and what information gaps, if any, cause hesitation?

  • Based only on what you see here, would you approve or deny this action? Tell me what made that decision easy or hard.
  • The agent has given you a reasoning trace and a confidence score. Is that enough — or do you need to verify something outside this screen before you'd click Approve?
  • What's the single piece of information you wish was on this screen that isn't?
Extends Task 3 Case detail RO-3 / RO-4
RQ-4B

How do users interpret and use the confidence trajectory chart when deciding whether to approve an escalated action — and does the visual representation support or complicate that judgement?

  • This chart shows the agent's confidence over time. What story does it tell you? Is that useful context for your decision?
  • The confidence dropped from 89% to 71% just before the agent escalated. What does that pattern tell you about why the escalation happened?
  • If you had to explain this chart to a non-technical colleague in 30 seconds, what would you say?
Net new Confidence chart Visual legibility
RQ-4C

Do users understand the reversibility of an approve or deny action — and does the absence of an undo mechanism create anxiety that delays or changes their decision?

  • If you click Approve and it turns out to be the wrong call — what happens? Can you undo it?
  • Does anything on this screen tell you whether this action is reversible or permanent?
  • Would knowing the action was reversible change how quickly you'd approve? How about knowing it was irreversible?
Net new Reversibility Decision anxiety
RQ-4D

When the system escalates to a user, do they understand the time pressure — if any — on their decision, and does the absence of a deadline or urgency signal affect how they prioritise the action?

  • How long do you think you have before this escalation expires or becomes irrelevant?
  • If you closed this panel and came back in 4 hours, would the situation be the same? How do you know?
  • Should this card look different if the issue is worsening while you review it? What would you want to see?
Net new Time urgency Escalation context
05

Additional aspects to consider testing

Gaps identified when mapping the four validation areas against the current prototype — not covered by the questions above or the existing UXR plan tasks.

Aspect Why it matters Suggested focus
Notification load & attention routing When multiple incidents are active simultaneously, do users know which to act on first? The canvas has three draggable cards with no clear priority hierarchy visible at a glance. Show two P1 and one P2 card simultaneously. Ask which they'd handle first and why.
Delegation intent — when users would turn off oversight The trust meter shows 5 autonomy levels, but the study doesn't ask users where they'd set their own threshold today — a critical adoption signal. After exploring the dashboard, ask: "At which autonomy level would you feel comfortable operating this in production? What would you need to move one level higher?"
Agent identity & accountability The prototype shows agent identity (name, version, policy, audit trail), but no task asks users whether they can identify which agent acted or who authorised a change — both critical for compliance. Ask users to find the agent responsible for a specific case outcome and identify who authorised its policy.
Onboarding / first-run comprehension All five tasks assume the user already understands what headless AI is. First-session impressions matter for adoption — do users grasp the paradigm shift before exploring details? Add a pre-task card sort or mental model elicitation before showing any screen: "Describe what you expect this tool to do in one sentence."
Cross-screen terminology consistency The system uses "guardrail," "policy," "permission," and "scope" sometimes interchangeably. Users may construct conflicting mental models depending on which screen they see first. After all tasks, show a card with five terms and ask users to rank them by how well they describe what they saw — note mismatches.
Failure & error recovery "Fix failed" appears in the case list but no task explores what users do next. If the agent fails, users need a clear recovery path — testing this prevents designing for success only. Create a scenario: the agent took an action that made things worse. Ask users to trace back the decision and describe what they'd change.

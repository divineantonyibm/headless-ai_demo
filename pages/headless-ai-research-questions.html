01

Slack escalation entry — first contact & handoff

Does the Slack card give users enough context to act directly — or confidently hand off to the IBM NI interface? Does the "Open in IBM NI" button feel like a natural next step?

Primary research questions
RQ-1A

When operators first encounter the IBM NI escalation card in Slack, do they understand what the system is asking them to do — and can they distinguish between "Approve recommended", "Open in IBM NI", and "Reject" without explanation?

  • Without reading anything aloud, tell me what you think this card is asking you to do.
  • What's the difference between clicking "Approve recommended" here and clicking "Open in IBM NI"? When would you choose each?
  • Is there anything on this card you'd want to check before clicking any button?
Net new Slack alert card Entry comprehension
RQ-1B

Do users understand the urgency communicated by the SLO breach timer ("SLO breach in 11 min") in the Slack card — and does it create appropriate pressure or anxiety that changes their decision-making behaviour?

  • What does "SLO breach in 11 min" mean to you — is that a deadline for you to act, or for the system to act?
  • If this said "SLO breach in 2 min" instead, would you behave differently? What would you do first?
  • Does this timer make you feel more confident or less confident about approving from Slack directly?
Net new SLO timer Urgency signal
RQ-1C

When clicking "Open in IBM NI" shows a "Connecting IBM Network Intelligence" loader before navigating, does the transition build confidence in the handoff — or does the delay introduce doubt?

  • You clicked the button and see a loading indicator. What do you think is happening? Does that feel expected?
  • How long would you wait before you assumed something had gone wrong?
  • Does the message "Connecting IBM Network Intelligence" tell you enough about what's happening, or would you want more detail?
Net new Connecting loader Transition trust
02

Decision Inbox — escalation review & plan approval

When the system surfaces a decision with recommended options, can users evaluate the plan, understand the steps, and approve or modify with confidence — without leaving the screen?

Primary research questions
RQ-2A

When operators are presented with the recommended options list in the Decision Inbox, can they make a confident approve or deny decision using only the information on screen — and what information gaps, if any, cause hesitation?

  • Based only on what you see here, would you approve option 1 or open one of the others? Tell me what made that decision easy or hard.
  • The agent has given you a reason why it escalated and a recommended option. Is that enough — or do you need to verify something outside this screen before you'd act?
  • What's the single piece of information you wish was on this screen that isn't?
Extends Task 3 Decision Inbox RO-3 / RO-4
RQ-2B

When a user clicks a recommended option and a plan popup appears showing execution steps and key parameters, does the plan content increase their confidence — and do they understand what will actually happen before clicking "Approve plan"?

  • You've clicked option 1 and a panel opened showing execution steps. Walk me through what you think will happen if you click "Approve plan".
  • Is the "What will happen" section clear enough to act on — or does it raise more questions than it answers?
  • What would make you close this popup and go back to re-read the case detail before approving?
Net new Plan popup Approval confidence
RQ-2C

When users click "Modify & approve" and the AI chat panel slides in from the right, do they understand that they can negotiate changes before approving — and does the chat interface feel appropriate for modifying an operational action?

  • You clicked "Modify & approve" and a chat opened. What do you think this is for — is it to ask questions, to change the action, or something else?
  • Looking at the suggestion chips — "Adjust pool size", "Change rollback window" — do those feel like the right options, or are there things you'd want to modify that aren't shown?
  • Would you trust a chat interface to make a parameter change to a production service? What would increase your trust here?
Net new Modify chat panel Negotiation UX
RQ-2D

Do users understand the time pressure communicated by the SLO countdown timer in the Decision Inbox — and does the timer's presence affect which option they choose or how quickly they act?

  • You can see a timer counting down here. What happens when it reaches zero — does the system act automatically, or does the case just expire?
  • If you closed this panel and came back in 15 minutes, would the situation be the same? How do you know?
  • Does the timer make you more likely to choose option 1 (the recommended one) rather than spending time reviewing options 2 or 3?
Net new SLO timer Decision urgency
03

Guardrail Studio — policy comprehension & change impact

Does the Guardrail Studio help users understand what the agent can do autonomously, what it is blocked from doing, and what changes when they edit a guardrail?

Primary research questions
RQ-3A

What understanding do IT operators and compliance officers develop about the agent's autonomous scope when reviewing the Guardrail Studio table — and does that understanding accurately reflect what the agent will and will not do?

  • Without being told, can you describe what this agent is allowed to do on its own right now — and what would cause it to escalate to you?
  • In the table, what does "Hard" versus "Soft" mean — and which type blocked the restart in the Decision Inbox scenario you just saw?
  • If you had to hand this guardrail configuration to a colleague, what would you warn them about?
Extends Task 4 Guardrail Studio RO-5
RQ-3B

When users open the Edit Guardrail drawer and adjust a setting, do they understand what downstream impact the change will have — and is the pre-flight preview (blast radius, rollback confidence) sufficient to support a confident save decision?

  • You've opened the edit drawer for guardrail G-01. What does changing the window time affect — just this rule, or something else in the system?
  • The drawer shows a rollback confidence score. What does that number tell you — and would a low score stop you from saving?
  • Would you feel confident clicking Save right now? What information is missing before you'd do that?
Net new Edit guardrail drawer Change impact
RQ-3C

Do users understand the relationship between the guardrail table and the Decision Inbox — specifically, that the case they just reviewed in the Inbox was caused by a hard guardrail visible in the Studio?

  • Looking at the guardrail table now, can you find the rule that caused the escalation you approved earlier? Point to it.
  • If you edited or disabled that guardrail now, what do you think would happen the next time the same situation occurred?
  • Does this screen feel connected to the Decision Inbox — or does it feel like a separate tool?
Net new Guardrail Studio Cross-screen coherence
04

Health Pulse — system legibility & notification routing

Does the Health Pulse tab give operators a clear, accurate sense of whether the system is running well — and do they understand when and why they will be notified?

Primary research questions
RQ-4A

What story do operators construct about overall system health from the Health Pulse hero panel — and which specific elements (fleet health %, confidence score, silence banner, log entries) drive their initial assessment?

  • In one sentence: is the system healthy right now? What on this screen made you say that?
  • The banner says "All systems nominal — running silently." Does silence feel like a positive signal, or does it make you want to look somewhere else to confirm?
  • What's the difference between "94% fleet health" and "95% confidence score" — do they tell you different things?
Extends Task 1 Health Pulse RO-2
RQ-4B

Do users understand the notification routing rules shown in the channel cards — specifically, when a Slack alert fires versus when something stays silent — and do they feel in control of when they will be interrupted?

  • Looking at the Slack channel card, what conditions will cause a message to be sent to #ops-alerts? Name as many as you can without reading the rules aloud first.
  • The recent log shows "Silent" for two entries and "Slack" for one. What rule caused the Slack one to be sent but not the others?
  • If you wanted to be notified for a specific type of event that isn't listed, where would you go to add that?
Net new Notification channels Routing logic
RQ-4C

When the recent notifications log shows a mix of auto-resolved, silent, and escalated entries, do users understand the distinction between them — and does the log reinforce or undermine their trust in the system's autonomous behaviour?

  • Looking at the activity log, which entries required your attention and which ones didn't? How can you tell?
  • The log shows 33 issues auto-resolved this week with no human involvement. Does that feel reassuring, surprising, or worrying?
  • If this log was empty, what would you conclude — that the system is healthy, or that something is wrong?
Net new Notification log Silent resolution trust
05

Remediation — workflow legibility & agent handoff

Does the Remediation tab communicate where a case is in its resolution lifecycle — and do users understand what the agent has done versus what still requires human action?

Primary research questions
RQ-5A

When operators view the Remediation stepper (Investigation → Ticket → Change Request → Maint. Window → Deploy), do they understand what has already happened autonomously versus what requires their involvement at each stage?

  • Looking at this stepper for INC-8841, which steps did the agent complete on its own — and which ones required a human to act?
  • The current step is "Deploy" and it's marked active. What does that mean you should do right now, if anything?
  • If you wanted to understand what happened during the "Change Request" step, where would you go?
Net new Remediation stepper Workflow legibility
RQ-5B

Do users understand the relationship between the Remediation case list (INC-8841, INC-8839, INC-8835) and the Decision Inbox — and do they perceive remediation as a continuation of the approval flow or as a separate tool?

  • INC-8841 appears in both the Decision Inbox and the Remediation tab. Are these the same case? How do they relate to each other?
  • If you approved an action in the Decision Inbox, where would you expect to track whether it worked?
  • The case list shows three incidents at different stages. Which one needs your attention most urgently — and how do you know?
Net new Remediation tab Cross-tab continuity
RQ-5C

When a canary deployment is in progress (25% traffic shifted), do users understand the risk and the rollback mechanism — and do they feel sufficiently informed to decide whether to proceed or stop?

  • The canary bar shows 25% traffic shifted. What does that mean in practice — and what happens if something goes wrong at this point?
  • How would you stop this deployment if you wanted to — and is that option visible on this screen?
  • At what percentage shifted would you start to feel nervous — and is there anything on this screen that would tell you whether to be nervous or not?
Net new Canary deployment Risk legibility
06

Additional aspects to consider testing

Gaps identified when mapping the five validation areas against the current prototype — not covered by the questions above or the existing UXR plan tasks.

Aspect Why it matters Suggested focus
Multi-decision triage in the Inbox The Decision Inbox shows 3 pending decisions simultaneously (⚡ Urgent, ⚠ Warning, one more). No task tests whether users understand the priority order or know which to act on first. Show the inbox with all 3 cards. Ask: "Which would you handle first and why?" — note whether the urgency tag, SLO timer, or card position drives the answer.
Reversibility awareness for approved actions The plan popup shows auto-revert conditions (e.g., "Pool reverts when connections drop below 300") but no task asks whether users understand that the action will reverse automatically — or whether they think it's permanent. After approving from the plan popup, ask: "When does this change go back to normal — and do you need to do anything to make that happen?"
Onboarding & mental model alignment All tasks assume users already understand what "headless AI" means. First-session impressions are critical for adoption — users arriving from Slack may have no context for what the IBM NI interface does. Before showing any screen, ask: "Describe what you expect this tool to do in one sentence." Then compare against their mental model after the session.
Tab navigation & orientation The interface has four tabs — Decision Inbox, Guardrail Studio, Health Pulse, Remediation. No task tests whether users understand what each tab is for or where to go for a given task without guidance. Ask users to find where they would check whether a previously approved action succeeded. Observe whether they navigate to Decision Inbox, Remediation, or Health Pulse first.
Modify chat panel — parameter trust The chat panel lets users adjust operational parameters (pool size, rollback window) via a conversational interface. It's unclear whether operators trust a chat UI for production changes, or expect a structured form. After the modify chat demo, ask: "Would you prefer this interface, a form with fields, or something else for making changes before approving? What would give you more confidence?"
Failure & error recovery The Health Pulse log shows "Autonomous action failed" as a possible notification trigger, but no task explores what users do when an agent action fails mid-remediation. Create a scenario: an approved pool bump failed to apply. Ask users to trace the failure, identify what happened, and describe what they'd do next.
Cross-screen terminology consistency The system uses "guardrail," "policy," "hard limit," "scope," and "permission" across different screens. Users may construct conflicting mental models depending on which tab they see first. After all tasks, show a card with six terms and ask users to match each to the screen where they first encountered it. Note mismatches between intended and perceived meaning.
